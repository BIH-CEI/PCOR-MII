PSS (Persistent Somatic Syndrome)

PSS (Persistent Somatic Syndrome) is the psychosomatic entity in PCOR-MII — alongside AN (Anorexia Nervosa) and NTx (kidney transplantation). The battery consists of generic instruments (identical across all three entities) and PSS-specific instruments on somatic symptom burden, health anxiety, mental comorbidity and healthcare utilisation.

The cross-entity view is on Instruments; this page describes the PSS battery and says, for every questionnaire, where its FHIR resource lives. Machine-readable, the same compilation is Library/use-case-pss, which pins all 23 questionnaires with versions. Rights and status of all instruments are collected on Rights and open points.

The PSS battery

The Resource column links to the resource itself: PCOR-MII — the Questionnaire is defined in this IG; MII PRO — the Questionnaire arrives through the package dependency de.medizininformatikinitiative.kerndatensatz.pros 2027.0.0-ballot.2, PCOR-MII does not rebuild it, the link goes to the artefact page of the published module; open — no artefact exists yet for this row of the data collection plan. The Example response column shows the PSS example patient's response at the screening visit of 25 June 2026 (see below). Priorities and frequencies are in the Domain Overview sheet of the Item Level Dictionary and in the display of the manifest entries.

The PHQ family in PSS

The PHQ-8 is the default — in PSS as in every use case: the eight depression items without the suicide item. That item is kept separately as PHQ-SI. The full PHQ-9 may only be collected when an on-site physician contact follows immediately — never in remote monitoring (see PHQ overview). Both are items of the same PHQ-9 definition:

Dictionary linkIds Meaning
PHQ-8 phq-phq2a–phq-phq2h Depressiveness without the suicide item
PHQ-SI phq-phq2i Suicidality — exactly the item that distinguishes PHQ-9 from PHQ-8

Whoever collects both effectively collects the full PHQ-9 — permissible only in a setting with an immediately following on-site physician contact. The split in the dictionary is an analysis and governance decision (never suicidality without a management plan or alert), not a content deviation. See PHQ overview.

The PHQ-D panic block (phq3a–phq3d) is something different from PHQ-4: four yes/no items on anxiety/panic attacks with a 4-week recall from block 3 of the PHQ-D. The MII PRO module has no artefact for it; PCOR-MII therefore maintains the block itself as PHQPANIK (ADR-012) — with linkIds in the PHQ-D namespace (phq-phq3a–phq-phq3d), so that a later adoption into the module needs no migration.

What distinguishes PSS from AN and NTx

  • PSS only: SSD-12, WI-7, SCOFF, ISR-Z, PC-PTSD, the PHQ-D panic block, EXPECT, IPQ-S, GSLTPAQ and the UKE healthcare-utilisation items. The Domain Overview lists PHQ-15 for PSS only (the NTx dictionary sheet includes it too — see NTx).
  • PSS and AN: OPD-SFK (collected by individual sites, no direct recommendation of the use-case lead) and PHQ-SI.
  • AN only: ERQ-6, EDE-Q6, ANSOCQ-2, SSUK-2, ACE and the UKHD supplementary questionnaires — see AN.
  • NTx only: BAASIS, ABQ, MTSOSD-R59, MHI-NTx and the NTx follow-up questionnaire — see NTx.

Rights and status

The PSS battery is for the most part freely publishable; the MII PRO instruments are governed there. Exceptions in PCOR-MII: WAI stays metadata-only until the German WAI network clears the German wording; OPD-SFK is free after consultation with the authors; in DEM and MHI the GI-PS items and four PaRIS third-party instruments are open. PROMIS and WHODAS 2.0 are subject to their usage agreements (CPCOR and WHO). The full situation per instrument and the open questions: Rights and open points.
