This page collects for all instruments together what is known about their rights situation, how they are modelled as a consequence, and which questions are still open — including the clinical points that cannot be solved in FHIR but in the Item Level Dictionary, with a site, or with the use-case lead. The use-case pages PSS, AN and NTx point here instead of repeating the situation per questionnaire; the design decisions (German) contain only what has been decided.

Answered questions disappear from this page: they become an ADR, a paragraph on the instrument page, or an entry in the release notes.

How the rights situation determines the modelling

The rights situation determines how an instrument is modelled — not how important it is:

  • free — complete questionnaire with item texts, answer options and scoring.
  • Usage agreement (PROMIS via CPCOR, WHODAS via WHO, EQ-5D-5L via EuroQol) — complete, but under the respective agreement; conditions in the copyright element.
  • metadata-only — no verbatim items or answers in the published package. Structure, linkIds, value ranges, score definition and how to obtain the instrument only. Applies to WAI, UKHD-EDP and BAASIS, ABQ and MTSOSD-R59. For the three licensed NTx questionnaires a private collectable variant is added per ADR-014, delivered to licensed centres only.
  • clearance open — the wording is included, but no documented permission is on record. This is not a rights status of its own but a state of knowledge: the DIZ implementation list covers published instruments only and says nothing about the site-specific item groups. Applies to the UKHD supplementary items, the MHH questionnaires MHI-NTx and NTx follow-up and the 34 site items already published in the MHI. If a restriction emerges, metadata-only per group is the intended remedy.

The source of the rights information is the DIZ implementation list PCOR-MII (as of 24 July 2026). Its licence column visibly records availability for research, not whether the wording may be passed on in a specification; for every instrument with a published German version the entry has therefore been checked independently. That is a working rule of this project, not legal advice.

Published is not the same as commercially published

Several of the open rights points hinge on the same distinction, which does not occur in the DIZ implementation list:

Published means: the instrument has been reported on — typically in a journal article describing construction, psychometrics or validation. The article is citable and often freely accessible. Whether the questionnaire itself may be reused is a different question.

Commercially published ("verlegt") means: the instrument is a publisher's product — manual, questionnaire and scoring sheet are distributed as a work. Here the wording is not a by-product of a publication but the goods themselves, with rights reservations that explicitly cover reuse of the wording.

In practice: freely accessible is not freely reusable. A publisher offering a PDF for download is a service, not a licence. Conversely an instrument may formally be published "only" in a journal and still be freely usable when the authors provide the questionnaire themselves.

Open rights questions

WAI — reassess publication rights

Status: open, added 2026-11-05 · Answer from: German WAI network (wai@uni-wuppertal.de)

The WAI is metadata-only (the pattern for ADR-012) because the DIZ implementation list notes "probably not suitable for publication". The note names no source. The terms of use of the originator, the Finnish Institute of Occupational Health (ttl.fi, accessed 2026-11-05), say the opposite: "The method is publicly available. You do not need permission from the Finnish Institute of Occupational Health to use the Work Ability Index." Use for research and one's own organisation is free, own questionnaires and translations are allowed. Conditions: cite the authors (Tuomi et al. 1998), do not sell the instrument as such, and give modified versions a name of their own; FIOH does not distribute the questionnaire itself.

One question remains: the German wording in the dictionary is the questionnaire of the German WAI network (INQA, University of Wuppertal, with the BAuA). According to the widely circulated questionnaire PDF, individual use is free and only use in companies requires consent; whether the translation may be passed on in a specification is not documented. The WAI is published and released by its originator, not commercially published.

Next steps: (1) ask the German WAI network whether the German wording of the three items may be published in the IG. (2) Independently, the verbatim questionnaire can be provided on the questionnaire server right away — that is use, not publication, and covered by the FIOH terms. (3) On a positive answer switch to the full version with citation and FIOH attribution in copyright; the short form stays named "Work Ability Score", as FIOH requires for modified versions. (4) Correct the DIZ list.

Site-specific item groups (UKHD, UKE, MHH) — clearance missing

Status: open, added 2026-09-30, refined 2026-10-01 and 2026-11-05 · Answer from: the Heidelberg, Hamburg and Hannover sites

The DIZ implementation list covers published instruments only. The site-specific item groups — Heidelberg (UKHD-*), Hamburg (UKE-*), Hannover (MHH-*) — do not occur there at all. For them neither a permission nor a restriction is documented: it is not a legal question the list could answer but a governance decision of the sites.

The dictionary contains 97 such variables. 34 of them PCOR-MII already publishes with full wording, all in the MHI: UKHD-W (4), UKHD-AN (1), UKHD-MEDI (2), UKE-MEDI (27).

Proposal: do not withhold wholesale, but distinguish two kinds. Trivial factual questions — body weight, AN subtype, medication list. "How much do you currently weigh in kg?" is not a protectable creation, and without the wording the DIZ cannot implement the items uniformly. These 34 remain published; the same holds for the MHH items in MHI-NTx and the NTx follow-up. Designed item batteries — UKHD-CTT (6, timing of childhood trauma), UKHD-BI (3, body image), UKHD-LE (4, life events), UKHD-ANB (2), UKHD-PT (2), UKHD-CT (1), UKHD-ND (3), UKHD_D (2). Here there is authorship from Heidelberg.

Seven of these groups are modelled — six as one Questionnaire each (UKHD supplementary items, one questionnaire per dictionary group per the site-group amendment in ADR-011) and UKHD-CTT inside the ACE, which thereby becomes a PCOR-MII composite. That is a deliberate project decision for piloting and not a clearance. The rights question itself has been refined: the UKHD prefix is a compilation label of the dictionary, not a statement of authorship — the manual of the master spreadsheet defines the INSTRUMENT column as the sites' choice of collection tool. Before the clearance question therefore comes a provenance question: where does the wording of each group come from — own phrasing, in-house documentation forms or a published instrument? The variable names give hints: most are descriptive study-database names (weight_discharge, life_event1_screening), where the risk is small. Three groups carry foreign, opaque abbreviation schemes, the signature of an external source: bdkm15/bdkm16 (UKHD-PT), erwEV24–erwEV26 (UKHD-BI) and edp1–edp11 (UKHD-EDP, see its own point). All resources carry status = draft, experimental = true and a copyright stating the reservation. If the feedback brings a restriction, the switch to metadata-only is made per group (pattern WAI); the example responses would be unaffected because they contain no item texts.

UKHD-BI is deliberately not modelled, and not for rights reasons: data collection does not yet use the visual picture scale, and the dictionary lists as answer option only a reference to a picture tab — without the picture template the item cannot be modelled, because the anchors of a visual scale are the object of measurement here, not labelling.

For UKE nothing is modelled (UKE-HCU/HCU2/PSE/TR/DOT, healthcare utilisation in PSS). For MHH the three screening items are modelled as MHI-NTx and the 18 monitoring items as the NTx follow-up questionnaire with full wording from the dictionary; open for MHH are the clearance of the wording, the group label for MHH_PTH1/MHH_PTH2 in the dictionary, and whether the groups are collected quarterly as one questionnaire or separately.

To clarify: confirmation by UKHD, UKE and MHH for both kinds — retroactively for the published factual items and prospectively for the batteries. Until then the status quo is an assumption, not a clearance.

UKHD-EDP — probably an EDI-2 subset, rights unassessed

Status: open, added 2026-09-30; modelled as metadata-only 2026-10-01 · Answer from: UKHD (identification), then Hogrefe

The collection plan lists 17 items for the domain Eating Disorder Psychopathology under the entry "EDEQ/EDP (UKHD)". These are two instruments, not one: the 6 items of the EDE-Q6 and 11 items with the variable IDs edp1–edp11. They cannot be merged — the EDE-Q asks "on how many of the last 28 days" on a frequency scale 0–6, the edp items ask timelessly for agreement ("1 = never true").

The UKHD prefix is misleading. The 11 items each represent one construct — drive for thinness, bulimia, body dissatisfaction, ineffectiveness, perfectionism, interpersonal distrust, interoceptive awareness, maturity fears, asceticism, impulse regulation, social insecurity — and those are the eleven subscales of the EDI-2 (Eating Disorder Inventory-2, Garner 1991; German version Paul & Thiel, Hogrefe 2005), one item per scale, i.e. the same subset pattern as EDE-Q6, ANSOCQ-2 and SSUK-2. A further indication: the answer format is six-point, as in the EDI-2; the other instruments of the AN battery use four, five or seven points. It is therefore very probably not an in-house development of the site but a subset of a commercially published test — published goods like the BDI-II, which the MII PRO module keeps as "data + scoring only, not displayable" for exactly that reason. The DIZ list has neither a row "EDI" nor "UKHD-EDP"; the rights situation is thus not "free" but not assessed at all.

Implemented as metadata-only, because that is right under both readings: if it is the EDI-2, the publisher's reservation forbids the wording; if it is a site original, clearance is missing. The displays of the eleven variables in the CodeSystem pcor-item-dictionary are neutralised as well.

To clarify, in this order: (1) confirm the identification as an EDI-2 subset against the German original questionnaire — edp8 and edp5 are characteristic. (2) On confirmation, update the DIZ list and clarify the rights with Hogrefe. (3) Only then assign Questionnaire.code or EDI-2 linkIds (ADR-008).

ACE-D — the German version is not freely publishable

Status: open, added 2026-09-30 — retroactively affects five published items · Answer from: Prof. Dr. Ingo Schäfer (University of Hamburg)

The DIZ implementation list lists the ACE as "freely available". That is true of the English original (Felitti et al. 1998, Kaiser Permanente / CDC), but the German version ACE-D is something else. Evidenced by the authors' own usage practice: the Deutsches Ärzteblatt prints, in a population-representative ACE study (Witt et al., Dtsch Arztebl Int 2019;116:635–42, doi:10.3238/arztebl.2019.0635), explicitly only two sample questions of ten in the supplement, with the note: "Copyright and citation: Ingo Schäfer, Katja Wingenfeld and Carsten Spitzer (2009) ACE-D … The complete questionnaire can be obtained from Prof. Dr. Ingo Schäfer." That is the pattern "available on request", not "freely publishable" — the same pattern as with the OPD-SFK, where the consultation has already been conducted successfully.

Consequences: (1) PCOR-MII already publishes the five German ACE items with full wording; the consultation is to be sought, following the OPD-SFK pattern. (2) An ACE-10 with German wording cannot be modelled from public sources; without consent only an English-primary version or metadata-only would be possible. (3) Positive side finding: the printed sample question on physical abuse is verbatim our ace2 — thus confirmed against a peer-reviewed source.

EDE-Q6 — clarify the rights of the German translation

Status: open, added 2026-09-29 · Answer from: Prof. Anja Hilbert (Leipzig University Medical Center)

The DIZ implementation list lists the instrument as "freely available", whereas the source publication explicitly reserves all rights. The wording used in PCOR-MII comes — verbatim, all six items and the answer scale — from Hilbert A, Tuschen-Caffier B, Eating Disorder Examination-Questionnaire. Deutschsprachige Übersetzung, 2nd ed., Tübingen: dgvt-Verlag 2016: "All rights reserved. […] This applies in particular to reproductions, translations, microfilming and storage and processing in electronic systems." (© 2016 Anja Hilbert). The last clause describes exactly what a FHIR Questionnaire does; that dgvt-Verlag offers the PDF for free download changes nothing.

EDE-Q6 is thus the third case of this kind, next to GI-PS and the already settled OPD-SFK. Same route: obtain the rights holder's consent, otherwise metadata-only per the WAI pattern. Questionnaire.copyright states the reservation until then; the licence column of the instrument overview says "confirmation sought" (ADR-006). Side finding: the DIZ list cites the Diagnostica publication of 2007 as the translation paper — that is the psychometric evaluation, not the translation; for wording questions the dgvt edition is authoritative.

Provenance rights of the DEM/MHI items

Status: OECD share settled ✅ (2026-09-24) · four PaRIS third-party instruments and GI-PS open · Answer from: rights holders of the GI-PS; check of the third-party instruments by the project

OECD (5 items in DEM) — settled. The items come from the OECD PaRIS Patient Questionnaire; the linkIds in DEM are the PaRIS variable names (Q_OECDLIT5a, Q_OECDLIT7a, Q_OECDLITii, OECDLIT2a, OECDLIT2b), the prefix stands for OECD Measuring Financial Literacy. The OECD Terms and Conditions permit non-commercial use and translation without explicit permission, provided the OECD is named as originator, the original is linked and the prescribed translation disclaimer is included; unlike PISA, PaRIS has no special conditions. The restriction in the DIZ list ("not published, for internal use only") describes the status of the translation, not the rights situation of the instrument. Questionnaire.copyright of DEM carries the provenance breakdown, the OECD attribution and the disclaimer. The German wording is the Swiss PaRIS version (Unisanté, Lausanne; recognisable by Helvetisms such as "einschliesslich" and "Ich weiss es nicht"), i.e. an official TRAPD translation, adopted unchanged per ADR-005.

Four PaRIS third-party instruments — check open. The PaRIS-PQ is itself a compilation, and the OECD explicitly excludes third-party content from its permission: "The User is responsible for verifying whether this is the case and, if so, securing the appropriate permissions."

It also remains sensible to compare the official German PaRIS translation (cApStAn, TRAPD, 16 languages) with the wording used — then the disclaimer reservation would fall away.

GI-PS (2 items in DEM, 9 items in MHI) — a genuine distribution restriction. Quote from the DIZ list: "The ownership, copyright, distribution and publication rights … remain with the respective test authors, i.e. the gi-ps or parts of it may not be modified, translated or passed on to third parties without the consent of the rights holders." (development paper: 10.13109/zptm.2023.69.1.56). Unlike the OECD there is no general permission: consent of the rights holders is required, otherwise the affected items are to be switched to metadata-only. Questionnaire.copyright of DEM and MHI states the reservation. The licence table on Instruments does not yet list OECD and GI-PS.

BAASIS, ABQ, MTSOSD-R59 — licensees, sources, versions

Status: open, added 2026-11-05 · Answer from: NTx use-case lead, MHH, University of Basel, KU Leuven

The three instruments are metadata-only with a private collectable variant (ADR-014); the rights are in the table above. Open:

  • Licensee: the University of Basel grants the BAASIS permission per project through its online form, the DIZ list speaks of a licence agreement per centre. This determines how the licence gate on the questionnaire server is cut (role per site or per project).
  • Data sharing: using the BAASIS obliges to share the data with the ongoing validation project of the University of Basel (Term IV). The use-case lead should know that before the licence request. Publications must cite Denhaerynck et al. 2023, Haupenthal et al. 2025 and the website.
  • BAASIS 2_a: the dictionary gives a rate scale for sub-question 2_a (from once to almost daily), the official explanation gives the same count categories for all three sub-questions. Whether the rate scale matches the licensed German questionnaire (version 2021) or is a local deviation is to be checked with MHH. Until then both scales are modelled separately.
  • ABQ: route to the licence agreement to be clarified. The polarity of the weights 1–4 is not verified against the original instrument — the dictionary lists the agreement level with value 1 as first option; whether the level order reflects barrier strength in the expected direction is to be checked before productive use. For ABQ_01 the dictionary names only BAASIS1A as trigger, for all other items BAASIS1A/2/3/4 — presumably an inconsistency.
  • MTSOSD-R59: whether MHH uses the German version linguistically validated by the MAPI Research Institute is to be confirmed. And the collection scope is open: according to the dictionary the complete questionnaire was collected, the Domain Overview sheet lists only the domains tremor and diarrhoea for monitoring (priority A, quarterly, telemonitoring weekly), the Comments and Questions sheet asks "complete side effects? Or separate?". The questionnaire is the basis in both cases; a telemonitoring subset of symptoms 16 and 30 would be derived as its own resource via derivedFrom (ADR-008).
  • PHQ-15 in NTx: the Domain Overview lists PHQ-15 for PSS only, the NTx dictionary sheet includes it; the NTx manifests therefore do not pin it. Decision of the use-case lead.

Further open points

PROMIS-GH — linkIds and continued existence of the 2-item questionnaire

Status: open, added 2026-11-11 · Decision: maintainer and use-case lead

Since 2027.0.0-ballot.2 the MII PRO module carries the complete PROMIS Scale v1.2 – Global Health (mii-qst-pro-promis-global-health, 10 items, status draft; German translation there so far only for Global01, Global02 and Global07). PROMISGH is declared via derivedFrom as its 2-item subset. Two questions: (1) the linkIds here are the dictionary variables Global01/Global02, upstream the same items are promis-global01/promis-global02 — ADR-008 would argue for the upstream IDs, which would be a breaking change for existing responses. (2) If the local questionnaire is dropped in favour of the module definition, the sites collect ten items instead of two — that is decided by the collection plan, not by the technology.

AN instruments — scoring, visual scales, dictionary erratum

Status: open (as of 2026-09-29; source verification of all five instruments completed) · Decision: AN use-case lead

  • Scoring: all five are subsets ("most discriminating item per scale" or "first 5 questions") for which no validated scoring rule exists. Deliberately no score item — until an evaluation rule is agreed; the answer codes carry itemWeight as a precaution. The former ERQ score has been withdrawn because the questionnaire is not the ERQ-S (see ERQ-6).
  • Visual scales: the answer scales of ERQ-6 (1–7) and EDE-Q item 27 (0–6) exist in the dictionary only as graphics (tabs "ERQ" / "EDE-Q27"); the anchors were taken from the graphics and are to be checked against the original questionnaires.
  • EDE-Q6 — erratum in the dictionary: the condition for edeq30 reads "If edeq31 = 1"; no variable edeq31 exists in the AN sheet. Implemented as enableWhen on edeq29 (the yes/no question directly before) — to be corrected in the dictionary.

UKHD supplementary items — open points on the dictionary

Status: open, added 2026-10-01 · Decision: UKHD and dictionary maintenance

While modelling the UKHD supplementary items, issues surfaced that cannot be solved in FHIR; they are also recorded as designNote on the respective item.

  1. Origin of the wording per group unresolved — see site groups; specifically to be asked for bdkm, erwEV and edp.
  2. bdkm15 and treatment_outpatient overlap. Both ask about current psychotherapeutic treatment — in two questionnaires, with two answer scales. They differ in time reference (bdkm15 only at admission and with history, treatment_outpatient at every visit and with setting), but at admission partly deliver the same information.
  3. The variable name treatment_outpatient is misleading. Level 4 of the answer scale explicitly captures inpatient or day-clinic treatment; the item asks about treatment status overall. The name stays as linkId and item.code but must not be read as a statement of meaning.
  4. comorbid1 — formally unclean, deliberately left (decided 2026-10-01). The question is literally a yes/no question, the dictionary provides a text field; the text field for further diagnoses is what is meant. type = text models the collection correctly, the wording stays unchanged per ADR-010.
  5. Inconsistent variable names in UKHD-LE. life_event1_screening/life_event1_monitoring versus lifev_discharge/lifev_text — two prefixes for one group, and no life_event2 exists; the example list of life_event1_screening apparently lacks the head of the phrase "break-up of a partnership".
  6. The group ID UKHD_D carries an underscore, all others a hyphen. Resource id and catalogue code follow the house convention (UKHD-D/ukhd-d), the dictionary spelling is preserved in the property instrument.
  7. bdkm16 asks about physician visits but sits under Past Treatment. In content the item belongs rather to healthcare utilisation (cf. UKE-HCU in PSS).
  8. UKHD-D overlaps with the MHI. There CPCOR-DIAG and GIPS13 collect as codes what is collected here as free text. Which representation is authoritative for the analysis is to be settled clinically.
  9. Highly sensitive content: UKHD-LE (loss experiences, abuse) and the group UKHD-CTT inside the ACE (childhood adversity) need, like the PHQ-SI, a governance of the evaluation.

PHQ-SADS — different recall for shared items

Status: open, MII PRO module roadmap 2027 (as of 2026-09-02) · Decision: MII PRO module

The PHQ-SADS is the joint evaluation of PHQ-15, GAD-7 and PHQ-9. In the shared PHQ-D namespace these are 31 items but only 29 distinct linkIds: phq-phq2c (sleep problems) and phq-phq2d (tiredness) belong to PHQ-15 and PHQ-9. PHQ-15 has a 4-week recall, PHQ-9 and GAD-7 a 2-week recall; the shared item is answered once but enters two scores with different time references. This cannot be solved at item level and must be reflected in the score interpretation. Responsible is the MII PRO module, where the PHQ questionnaires, the namespace and the score ObservationDefinitions are maintained; PCOR-MII documents the conflict in the "PHQ-SADS" section of the PHQ overview and adopts the solution once it is taken upstream.

Rows of the collection plan not yet modelled

PROMIS NRS Pain Intensity 1a as a stand-alone instrument (the item is inside PROMIS-29), the summary scores PHS/PCS/MHS/MCS (priority A, no artefact), the PROMIS-16 domain T-scores (upstream roadmap 2027), the UKE healthcare-utilisation items in PSS and UKHD-BI in AN. Details in the tables of the use-case pages.
